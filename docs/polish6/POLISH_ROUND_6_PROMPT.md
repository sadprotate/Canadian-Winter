### 3. Learn the game before you touch it

**What it is.** A first-person snow-removal game set on a few streets of a Canadian town, from
December 1 to March 31, one winter after another.
- **The snow:** a real heightfield, 25 cm cells over the whole map. You scoop, throw and push it
  with your hands and tools, and later clear it with machines and vehicles. Snow volume is
  conserved.
- **The opening:** you dig out after a storm, find your shovel and truck keys, and help the
  neighbour Gerry clear his walk; he unlocks your computer.
- **The business:** you bid on clearing contracts (residential, then commercial, then city), buy
  tools, upgrades and equipment, pay hydro bills, and follow the forecast on the TV, the radio and
  the computer.
- **The modes:** Fun Mode has no cold. Survival Mode adds warmth and freezing.
- **Time:** a game day is 30 real minutes. Sleeping skips to 7:00 a.m. and saves.
- **Assets:** everything is procedural: meshes, shaders, sounds, icons.

## Decisions (already made — apply them)

1. **Bigger loads (§8).** "Shovels that can pick up and throw" means every hand tool that scoops
   and throws: small shovel, standard shovel, wide pusher, snow scraper, large scraper, snow
   sleigh. Their base **capacity doubles**. Bare hands, the electric shovel and the blowers don't
   change. Throw distances and push values don't change.
2. **Smooth piles (§9).** Applies to snow the player moves: hand pushes, every plow blade, the
   pickup's shove, back-drag and thrown snow landing in a pile. **The city grader's pass stays
   exactly as it is** (round 5 Decision 6: the playtester likes its rough street). Volume is
   conserved exactly.
3. **The plow truck from the start (§10).** A new game starts with the pickup **with its front
   plow fitted** (the existing `pickup_plow` vehicle), where the plain pickup starts now, snowed in
   as now. The keys are still buried under the lawn tree and still needed. The front plow is no
   longer sold. With the blade up, the truck drives and shoves through snow at least as well as
   round 5's plain pickup.
   - **Old saves:** a plain pickup gets the plow fitted on load, free, with one notification. A
     front-plow order that is still pending is cancelled and refunded. Nothing else changes.
   - **Contracts:** the starting truck **doesn't raise `Contracts.equipment_capability()`**
     (it counts as hand tools), so the early listings keep their size. Bought plow or blower
     vehicles count as now. It does satisfy the "plow or blower vehicle" requirement of street
     listings (they stay locked by reputation).
4. **Blade up/down and back-drag (§11).** Two new vehicle actions, `veh_blade_up` (Up arrow) and
   `veh_blade_down` (Down arrow), for every plow vehicle the player drives (pickup with plow, ATV,
   tractor blade, plow truck). The blade keeps its two positions, up and down. The existing
   `veh_tool` toggle stays as it is. **Pull** means back-dragging: with the blade down and the
   vehicle reversing, the blade drags snow backward with it, and lifting the blade leaves it as a
   pile. It uses the existing blade op, and volume is conserved.
5. **Every key rebindable (§12).** Every action the game reads is in `Settings.ACTIONS` and can be
   rebound in the settings menu, in every context, with the conflict check. Godot's built-in
   `ui_*` actions (menu navigation, Esc to back out of a field) stay as they are and are listed in
   the report.
6. **The phone (§13).** An equipment item (round 5 §13b's kind: store crate, never a tool slot, one
   of each), sold in the Equipment store at a balance price (start around $120). Picked up at the
   store like any order. Opened and closed with a new `phone` action (default **P**). It has three
   apps: **Contract board**, **Equipment store** and **Radar**. The board and the store reuse the
   computer's code (one implementation of bidding and buying). It works on foot, indoors and in a
   stopped vehicle, in both modes, and in a power cut. While it's open the player stands still and
   the world keeps running, as at the computer.
7. **The radar (§13) never knows more than the radio.** Frames for hours that have passed show
   what fell. The current frame shows what is falling now. Up to `Weather.RADIO_WINDOW_H` (3) game
   hours ahead, the frames show the radio's forecast (`fc_hour(…, radio = true)`). An unforecast
   major event appears on the radar exactly when it appears on the radio, never earlier.
8. **Weather you can see is visual and audio only (§7).** What falls stays bit-identical
   (`weather_truth`). Nothing new reads the truth ahead of the current hour. Wind gusts are visual
   and audio only; exposure still reads the hourly wind.
9. **Round 5 §14–§16** are done as written in the round-5 brief, with the updates in Part 3.
10. **Hold to fill, never click (§8).**
    - **Snow is picked up only while the scoop button is held** and the tool moves through the
      snow, or stays held in place for whole dig cycles. A press doesn't cut anything by itself.
    - **Every tool that scoops has a throw rhythm:** after a throw it needs a recovery time before
      it can scoop again. This is a balance value per tool, from about 0.5 s for bare hands to
      about 1 s for the sleigh.
    - **Clicks are ignored, not queued:** during recovery, and when a release comes before a
      minimum hold.
    - **The result:** spam-clicking never moves snow faster than holding and sweeping, at any click
      rate, with any tool: bare hands, every shovel-type tool, the electric shovel, the blowers.
    - **The press cap stays:** round 5's per-second cap (`input_max_presses_per_s`, 6) stays as it
      is for every action.

## 2. The Godot errors after pulling

The user pulled `main` into an existing checkout, opened the editor, and got:

```

ERROR: res://scripts/ui/icons.gd:125 - Parse Error: Identifier "ToolIcons" not declared in the current scope.
ERROR: res://scripts/ui/icons.gd:140 - Parse Error: Identifier "Equipment" not declared in the current scope.
ERROR: modules/gdscript/gdscript_resource_format.cpp:46 - Failed to load script "res://scripts/ui/icons.gd" with error "Parse error".
```

**What's known.**
- Both classes exist on `main`: `scripts/ui/tool_icons.gd` (`class_name ToolIcons`, round 5 §6)
  and `scripts/data/equipment.gd` (`class_name Equipment`, §13b).
- CI's clean import of `1b38f4a` (no `.godot/`) passes with no errors.
- `icons.gd` (`class_name Icons`) existed before round 5. Lines 125 and 140 are its only
  references to classes round 5 added.
- Round 5 added nine `class_name`s in all: `AddressSigns`, `Buildings`, `Equipment`, `HeatedPad`,
  `Heating`, `ItemUpgrades`, `SidewalkCrew`, `SpeedControl`, `ToolIcons`.

**Most likely cause.** The global class cache in the user's `.godot/`
(`global_script_class_cache.cfg`) was written by the pre-pull build. The editor loaded `icons.gd`
before its file-system scan had registered the new classes: Godot was open during the pull, or
the script was open in a tab. CI never hits this because it always imports from a clean tree.

**Tasks.**
- [ ] **Reproduce.** In a scratch clone: check out `15be65a` and import

  A missing `.uid`.
- [ ] **Harden.** Every script that existed before round 5 and references a class added in
  round 5 refers to it through a `preload` constant
  (`const _ToolIcons := preload("res://scripts/ui/tool_icons.gd")`) instead of the global name. The

- [ ] **CI: an upgrade import.** Add a step that imports the PR's base commit (keeping `.godot/`),

## 8. Shovelling: hold to fill, no machine gun, loads twice as big

### 8.1 Hold to fill, not spam-click

- [ ] Snow is picked up while holding the mouse button, not by spam-clicking.
- [ ] Each tool allows only a set number of scoop-and-throw actions per second, so no tool fires
  snow like a machine gun.

**What's wrong today** (round 5 report s2, §2.3; `tests/click_bench.tscn` measures it):
- Round 5 capped world-changing presses at 6 per second (`Settings.accept_press`,
  `input_max_presses_per_s`). With bare hands, holding and sweeping removes 0.041 m³/s, but
  clicking at the cap still removes 0.071–0.078 m³/s, about 1.8× as much.
- **Why:** every press opens a new stroke, and `ToolController._fill()` resets `_dig_timer` and
  `_cut_time` at the start of every stroke. The first frame of each press therefore digs a lump of
  `fill_rate × cycle_time × 0.4` in place: about 15 L for bare hands, half a load, where sweeping
  takes about 1.4 L per frame. Every release then throws (`_release_throw`).
- So each click is a free half-scoop and a throw. Round 5 wasn't allowed to change how a stroke
  cuts, so the cap was all it could do.
- Round 5's reverted experiment letting the dig cycle run on across presses took clicking below
  sweeping. But the hold-and-sweep bot lost about 7 % (path box 3.2 → 3.7 min) because its quick
  strokes used the opening bite too.

**Measure first.** Run `click_bench` at 5, 10, 20 and 50 clicks per second, plus hold-and-sweep,
for bare hands, all six shovel-type tools, the electric shovel and both walk-behind blowers.
Record the snow removed per second, the throws per second, and anything a click does that holding
doesn't. Do the same for RMB pushing (round 5 found no per-press gain there; confirm it).

**Then (Decision 10):**
- **No opening bite.**
  - A press cuts nothing by itself. Snow comes into the tool only as the scoop sweeps through it,
    at the fill rate, frame by frame.
  - Holding still digs in place, one bite per full `cycle_time` of holding. The first in-place
    bite comes after one full cycle, not on the press.
  - The re-cut memory isn't wiped by a new press. Releasing and pressing again over the same
    cells doesn't renew their bite early.
- **A throw rhythm.**
  - A release that throws starts a recovery: `throw_recovery_s`, a new `ToolData` value per tool,
    in `data/tools/*.tres` (and in the generator, §1). Start around 0.5 s for bare hands, 0.7 s for
    the small and standard shovels, 0.8 s for the pusher and scrapers, and 1.0 s for the sleigh.
  - The throw's follow-through plays out during recovery. No scoop can start until it ends.
  - Presses during recovery are ignored, not queued, with no error sound. Holding through the end
    of recovery starts the scoop then, so a player who keeps the button down never loses time.
- **A minimum hold.** A release that comes less than `min_scoop_hold_s` (balance, around 0.25 s)
  after its press throws nothing. The load stays in the tool, and nothing is lost.
- **Machines.** For the electric shovel and the blowers, starting and stopping by clicking must give
  no burst that holding doesn't: no start-up intake lump, no extra throw per press. They throw
  continuously while held, as now.
- **Holding stays as good as it is.**
  - The hold-and-sweep rate (the `grind_bench` bot) stays within 5 % of baseline, and a human
    rhythm (hold and sweep 0.6–1.2 s, release, repeat) loses nothing.
  - If removing the opening bite slows sweeping, make up for it in the sweep fill, not by giving
    clicks anything back.
  - Report `grind_bench` before → after.
- **Unchanged:** the press cap of 6 per second for every action, push behaviour, throw distances,
  where the indicator shows the cut and the landing, and the poses' grip points.
- **Help text and the tool-pickup hint** say "hold" (they already do; check the wording still

## Rules the code depends on
- **Snow volume is conserved exactly.** The only exceptions are ledgered: box completion (the last
  ≤ 10 % at the 90 % snap), melting (`Snow.melted`), the dump truck, and setup/debug fills. Never
  loosen a tolerance to make a test pass.
- **The weather that falls is fixed.** `weather_truth` must stay identical; only the forecasts may
  change, and only when a brief says so.
- **Randomness.** Anything new that's random gets its own seeded `RandomNumberGenerator`. Never call
  the global `randf()` / `randi()` in new code, and never add or reorder draws in an existing
  stream.
- **Time.** `Clock` is the only clock; never hard-code its rate. Anything that runs on the clock
  steps inside its per-step tick, so 1×, 2× and 10× give identical results.
- **Input.** Every action is in `Settings.ACTIONS` (rebindable, conflict-checked). World-changing
  presses go through `Settings.accept_press()`. Every key shown to the player comes from
  `Settings.action_label()` / `Settings.with_keys()`. Anything that reads keys checks
  `Game.typing()`.
- **Buildings.** New buildings call `Buildings.register()`. Snow work checks
  `ToolController.can_work_at()`.
- **Saves** are version 3: migrations in `Saves`, the atomic write and `.bak` untouched, every new
  key optional with a safe default and added to the v3 round-trip test. Derive state where you can.
  Fast-forward is never saved.
- **Balance values** live in `data/balance.tres` and the defaults in
  `scripts/data/balance_data.gd`; keep them in sync. Use `tools/data_gen/generate_data.gd` only if
  it reproduces the current data exactly.
- **New scripts:** run the import so their `.uid` is generated, and commit it. A script that
  existed before a `class_name` was added refers to that class through a `preload` constant: the
  editor's class cache on the owner's PC can be stale after a pull.
- **Render-to-texture:** a `SubViewport` renders once or when its content changes, never per frame,
  with a headless fallback.
- **Quitting:** `AudioMgr.quit(code)`, never `get_tree().quit()`.
- **Tests:** new tests go in the round's suite. A test that asserts behaviour a brief deliberately
  changes is updated through lookups (layout, balance, `Clock`), never deleted, skipped or
  loosened. Don't touch unrelated tests.
