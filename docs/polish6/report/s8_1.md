## Decision needed: the throw rhythm against "holding stays as good as it is"

Two items can't be met with Decision 10's recoveries, and no change within §8.1's rules meets
them. Both have the same cause. After a throw, no scoop can start for `throw_recovery_s` (0.5 s for
bare hands up to 1.0 s for the sleigh), and a press made in that time waits for it to end.

**1. grind_bench within 5 %.** The bot turns, throws and turns back in 8 frames. Every stroke
therefore waits out the rest of the recovery: 22 frames for bare hands.

- **B. Shorter recoveries.** With every tool at 0.4 s (measured, 0.3 s between strokes): bare

## What clicking did that holding didn't (measured first)

`tests/click_bench` now runs every tool (`--tools=all`): bare hands, the six shovel-type tools, the
electric shovel and both walk-behind blowers. It runs them through the real input map at 5, 10, 20
and 50 clicks a second, next to holding.

- **Bare hands and every shovel-type tool, before.**
  - Each registered press opened with an in-place bite in its first frame (bite on press:
    13–17 L for hands, up to 68 L for the sleigh) and threw on release.
  - Clicked in place at the cap, hands removed 0.061–0.081 m³/s against 0.041 for the grind bot
    (1.5–2×). Against their own hold: the small shovel 1.7–2.2×, the standard shovel 1.1–1.5×,
    the pusher 1.3–1.7×, the snow scraper 1.4–1.8×, the large scraper 1.0–1.4×.
  - Clicking while sweeping was about as strong.
  - The sleigh was the exception: its 1 m throw lands back in the box.
  - Throws came up to 6 a second (5.1–5.8/s at 10–50/s), never closer than 0.05 s apart.
- **Electric shovel, before.** No intake lump: the first frame takes one frame's intake (0.8 L).
  But every release threw at once, and a press could throw on its first frame. Clicked, it threw
  up to 7.2 times a second, gaps down to 0.03 s, against 6.06/s held. That is an extra throw per
  press, with no more snow.
- **Blowers, before.** No burst: one frame's intake on the press (1.1 / 2.3 L), and the chute's
  0.18 s throw timer only runs while running. Clicking removes about half of holding.
- **RMB, before.** Bare hands: no per-press gain, as round 5 found (0.0066 m³/s pushed at every
  rate). Other tools in the opening's ~0.6 m path can push more when clicked, because a held push
  stalls against its berm (speed ×0.01) while clicking lets the player wade on between presses.
  It varies from run to run, in both builds (open item). Push behaviour is protected in §8.1.

  | blowers | 0.18 (their chute interval, was a constant) |

- `min_scoop_hold_s` = 0.25 in `balance.tres` and `BalanceData`, plus a record line in the
  generator. The generator still reproduces `data/` (`test_s1_data_generator_reproduces_data`),
  and `balance.tres` still equals the defaults.

**`ToolController`: hands and shovel-type tools**
- **No opening bite.**
  - A stroke starts with `_dig_timer = cycle_time`, not 0. Held still, the first in-place bite
    comes one full cycle after the press.
  - `_cut_time` is no longer cleared at each stroke, so the re-cut memory survives a new press.
- **One in-place bite per cycle of holding, the sweep's intake included (review fix).**
  - The dig cycle runs while the button is held, sweeping or not.
  - What the sweep took during the cycle (`_cycle_swept`) counts toward the cycle's in-place bite.
  - In the first commit, the cycle ran on during the sweep, and the first still frame after one cycle
    took a whole in-place bite on top of the sweep's fill, in one frame. That is a still frame
    where the aim moves under 4 mm, as at a real mouse's turn-back. The bite was up to 17 L for
    bare hands, 27 L for the standard shovel and 77 L for the sleigh.
  - `test_s81_a_pause_in_a_sweep_adds_no_lump` failed on the first commit: a 26.6 L lump on the
    sleigh's first still frame. It passes now: nothing until a full cycle of stillness, then one
    76.8 L bite.
  - It can't help clicks either way: the cycle restarts with each scoop, and a cycle (≥ 0.4 s) is
    longer than the minimum hold.
- **The footprint only when the strip comes up short, at most the opening bite's share a load
  (review fixes).** A sweep frame cuts the strip it crossed, as before §8.1. Only when the strip
  yields less than the frame's fill does the rest come from the scoop's footprint where it is now:
  the cells digging in place takes. In all, for a load, it takes at most the opening bite's size,
  `fill_rate × cycle_time × 0.4` (`_foot_left`), and only on the load's first stroke: a stroke
  that goes on filling a kept load gets none, so a click can't renew it. `_cut_cells()` does each
  cut, and the fill rate still caps every frame.
  - This is the "make up for it in the sweep fill" the brief allows. With no footprint at all, the
    bare-hands dig-out from the porch (`test_dig_out_by_hand_after_a_major_event`) reached 0.6 m
    of the 3.2 m. The bot's sweep there crosses a 0.13 m arc in a narrow trench, and only the
    opening bite's wider in-place box ever reached the trench walls (probe traces, before and
    after).
  - First commit (`065a487`): the footprint was cut on every sweep frame. It spans the blade's
    width sideways, so it took part of each load from beside the strip, and near the box's edge

## Readings taken

- **The minimum hold counts from the physical press,** not from the scoop's start after a
  recovery. The brief: "A release that comes less than min_scoop_hold_s after its press". It can't
  make a throw sooner: a throw needs a load, and the load needs the scoop, which waits for the
  recovery. The other reading (count from the scoop's start, which is when the cap registers the
  press) is option D in *Decision needed*: measured, not taken, for the coordinator to pick.
- **The dig cycle runs while held, sweeping or not** ("one bite per full cycle_time of holding").
  It used to count only still frames, and the opening bite reset it. Held still from the press,
  the first bite still comes one cycle after the press.
  - With the old still-only count, the polish 4 indicator test's first-person pusher and scrapers
    filled only near the end of its 40-frame look-up. The landing ring hadn't finished gliding when
    they released, so the test got 35 qualifying throws of 36.
  - **Its effect in the middle of a sweep** (first review): in the first commit, the first still frame after a
    cycle of sweeping took a whole in-place bite on top of the sweep. The bots' sine sweeps rarely
    make such frames, so no bench measured it. Now what the sweep took counts toward the cycle's
    bite (*What changed*). The indicator test still gets 37 throws.
- **How much and where a sweep cuts** (round 5 protects it; the brief allows making up the opening
  bite in the sweep fill). A frame whose strip fills it cuts only the strip, as before §8.1. A
  frame whose strip comes up short takes the rest from the scoop's footprint, at most the opening
  bite's share a load (*What changed*).
  - **Corrected (second review):** the first amendment said a sweep through open snow cuts exactly
    as before. That is true in deep snow only. In snow too shallow for the tool to fill up within
    a second, the strip comes up short on every frame, and a held sweep takes up to about a quarter
    more than before §8.1, from up to four more cells (*Shallow snow*). For the coordinator to
    accept.
  - The first commit cut the footprint on every sweep frame, and the wide tools' loads came partly
    from beside the strip.
- **The recovery belongs to the player, not the tool.** It runs on across a tool switch, so two
  tools can't alternate to double the rhythm. A press held through the switch keeps waiting.
- **The machines' "throw recovery" is their chute interval:** 0.15 s (electric) and 0.18 s
  (blowers), the values the code used.
  - Held, they still throw continuously.
  - The electric shovel's held throws now come every 10 frames instead of 9: the beat is counted
    once per frame, held or not, and float rounding pushes it one frame. Same count in 18 s.
- **The landing indicator** shows the cells the scoop cut, the footprint included when it cuts
  there. The indicator test passes, with the worst offset at 0.15 m or less.
- **`click_bench` resets** the throw recovery and the per-second press history (`Settings._presses`)
  between runs. Without that, a machine run straight after 50/s clicks found its hold press
  refused by the cap. Its "removed" counts snow still in the tool, flagged ¹.
- **"Removed" depends on more than the scoop:** it also counts where the snow came from and where
  it landed. The click bench's in-place click rows remove 0.002–0.004 m³/s with nothing scooped:
  that is snow the wading bot treads out of the box.
