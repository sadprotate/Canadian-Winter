again.

|---|---|---|---|

What makes the peaks:
- **The deposit.** `blade_push` puts every cell's snow on one line, `push_dist + randf() × 0.12`
  ahead of the face. A berm grows as a one-cell ridge; a plow stacks it to the 6 m ceiling between
  relaxation passes. While pushing, the relaxation (time-budgeted) can't keep up, so the berm is
  spiky as you push, and the pusher stops when the spike reaches hip height.
- **The relaxation, in two ways.** (1) The guard (the strip behind an engaged blade is a wall for
  slumps) leaves a cliff: tiles relaxed while guarded found nothing to move and were never queued
  again, so once the blade had gone the pile kept its blade-side face until it hardened (30 h)
  and froze there. Re-queuing the area brought it down. (2) Settled loose snow is a 43° tent or
  cone meeting in a one-cell point: the relaxation only limits slopes.
- **Not the renderer.** Its normals are already per vertex, central differences over two cells,
  so shading is smooth; piles near the player are drawn at full resolution (LOD 1), and the skirts
  only hang under chunk edges. The faceted look came from the geometry: one-cell ridges, cliffs and
  flat 43° faces meeting at a point. Not changed.

## What changed

- **The band (`Snow.blade_push`).** Each moved cell's snow lands over five points along the face
  normal (`PUSH_BAND`, Hann weights 1-3-4-3-1 / 12), `min(0.2, push_dist / 3)` apart and centred
  where the line was (push_dist plus the same 0–12 cm draw): bare hands 0.10–0.50 m ahead, the
  hand tools 0.13–0.63, the plows 0.20–1.12, the pickup's shove 1.10–2.02. Nothing lands behind
  the face. Still exactly one draw from `push_rng` per moved cell (§3); `test_s9_blade_push_spreads_over_a_band`
  checks the draws, the rows (3–5, was one line) and the centre (within 0.1 m of the old line's).
  The ceiling and the curb are handled per point as they were per cell. The cells it marks dirty
  and queues for relaxation cover every point it reaches (the shove's far points could fall
  outside the old rectangle).
- **The guard's tiles settle after the blade.** `set_blade_guard` notes the tiles its strip covers
  (and those beside it); each is queued once the guard no longer covers it, and all of them
  `GUARD_SETTLE_STEPS` (45) relaxation steps — 0.75 s at 1× — after the guard has expired without
  being set again (`guard_tiles`, `guard_settle`, `_release_guard`). The wait matters: released at
  once, a push made of quick presses let the berm slump back onto the cleared strip between
  presses, to be pushed again by the next, and polish 5's `test_s2_autoclick_capped` failed —
  clicking RMB at 50/s pushed 0.150 m³ against holding's 0.099. With the wait it pushes 0.099,
  as before.
- **Player-moved snow (`Snow.soft`).** A byte per cell: the loose snow on it was pushed (hand
  tools, every plow blade, the pickup's shove, so §11's back-drag too), thrown or dumped
  (`deposit`, and its ceiling overflow through `_spill`), or slid off such snow. Not marked: the
  city grader's windrows, the sidewalk crew's mounds, wading's flanks (`deposit(…, moved = false)`)
  and setup fills. It clears once the cell holds no more than 2 mm of loose snow, whatever took
  it: at its chunk's next sync (`sync_chunk`, a pass over the chunk's tiles that hold marks:
  hardening, melting, a scrape, a slump that drained it, the save's 1 mm rounding), and at once
  where `set_rect` clears loose snow (a box's completion snap). Such snow relaxes by two rules, in
  a pass of its own (`_relax_tile_soft`), taken only by tiles that hold some (`soft_tiles`, a count
  per tile kept exact by `_set_soft` / `_clear_soft`); every other tile runs the original loop
  unchanged:
  - it slumps past **`moved_repose_max_diff` = 0.19 m a cell (37°)**, against `repose_max_diff`'s
    0.23 (43°), which every other loose snow keeps;
  - **its tops round off:** a cell may stand above its lower neighbour no more than
    `2 × moved_crest_max_diff` (0.16 m) more than it stands below its neighbour on the other side,
    i.e. never more than 0.08 m above the two's mean. Whatever stops snow sliding onto that other
    neighbour supports the cell like a wall: a wall, the map's edge, the curb from the property
    side, a completed box from outside it, the guarded strip. So a pile against a wall or the curb
    keeps its slope there.
- **Saved.** `serialize_pending()` adds `"soft"` (zstd-compressed bytes as base64) to the save's
  snow, read back by `deserialize()`; a save without it has no marks, so an older save's loose snow
  settles exactly as before (and §3's settle on load is unchanged). In the v3 round-trip test.
- **Balance:** `moved_repose_max_diff` 0.19 and `moved_crest_max_diff` 0.08 (`balance_data.gd`
  defaults, `balance.tres`).
- **Counters:** `SnowRenderer.mesh_us_last` (µs the last frame spent on chunk meshes, beside
  round 5's `relax_us_last`); `perf.tscn` prints a "snow counters" line after each scenario
  (relaxation and chunk mesh µs p95 / max, chunk updates a frame).
- **`shot.gd --pile=hand|plow|pickup|throws`** (see its header).

Unchanged: conservation; the cut walls of undisturbed snow (only loose snow moves, as before);
completed boxes as retaining walls; the grader's lane op, its street and the sidewalk crew; the 6 m
ceiling and its spill; relaxation's and the meshes' frame budgets; the renderer.

### Choosing the values

`moved_crest_max_diff` sets how round the tops are. With 0.03 a big pile is a true dome (the
thrown pile 0.94 m, its top curving over ±0.75 m), but the rule is scale-free, so a small pile is
all top: bare hands pushing 20 cm of snow (`test_push_builds_a_mound_conserving_volume`) left a
28 cm mound, under the test's 30 cm. Measured with that test: 0.05 → 0.30 m, 0.06 → 0.31, 0.07 →
0.33, 0.08 → 0.34 (0.33 in the final run). 0.08 rounds a big pile's top over one to two cells
(it blunts the cone; the shots show a rounded top) and passes with margin; it is a balance value
if the owner wants rounder piles and lower hand mounds. 0.19 (37°) for the step is "a little lower
than 43°".

## Review (amended)

An independent review passed §9 with four minor findings, fixed in this commit:
1. **Stale marks.** A mark was cleared only where its cell's loose snow hardened, so a cell emptied
   another way (a box's completion snap, the heated pad's melting, a floor-0 scrape, a slump that
   drained it, the sidewalk crew, the save's 1 mm rounding) kept it for the rest of the winter, and
   snow that wasn't the player's landing there later (wading's flanks, the crew's mounds, the
   city's windrows) settled as player-moved snow and spread the mark. Now a mark goes whenever its
   cell holds no more than 2 mm of loose snow: at the chunk's next sync, and at once in `set_rect`
   (*What changed*; `test_s9_emptied_cells_lose_their_marks`). Tiles without marks relax exactly
   as before; the grader's pass and `test_s11_grader_pass_unchanged` are untouched.
