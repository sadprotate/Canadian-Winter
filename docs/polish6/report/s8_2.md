- *fill rate*: the frame took the whole `fill_rate × dt`;

How to read it:
- **§8.1: what limited a stroke.**
  - The pusher, the scrapers, and the shovels in 40–80 cm were capacity-limited: most strokes
    filled up (17–30 of 30). Where they didn't, they were limited by the snow the sweep crossed.
  - The sleigh never filled its 400 L in a stroke. It took its whole sweep's snow
    (bite × width × the arc of the sweep): 140 L.
  - In 20 cm the shovels and the sleigh were limited by the depth (10–12 of 30 shovel strokes full);
    the pusher and the scrapers, whose bites are under 20 cm, still mostly filled up.
  - The fill rate alone limited no tool. It sets how fast the fresh part of a sweep fills.
- **Doubling capacity and fill rate alone** (column 5) gave ×1.2–×1.6 for the shovels,
  ×1.2–×2.1 for the pusher and the scrapers (×1.5–×1.9 from 40 cm), and ×1.04–×1.17 for the sleigh.
  With twice the room, the strokes became limited by the snow the sweep crosses: for the shovels
  and the sleigh, "nothing" frames rose to 50–73 % (§8.1: 25–58 %).
- **With §8.2's bites** (column 6): ×1.83–×1.93 in 80 cm, and ×1.71–×2.08 in 40 cm except the
  standard shovel's ×1.57.
- **Strokes a minute barely change** (42–46 for the shovels, 40 for the pusher and scrapers, 35
  for the sleigh). §8.1's recovery sets the rhythm, so the thrown m³/s rises with the load:
  ×1.3–×2.0 (the sleigh in 20 cm ×1.1).
- **The baseline (before §8.1)** stroked about twice as often with the same load. §8.2 doesn't
  bring that back; it brings the removal rate back near it (*The ladder*, the path-box table).

- **The standard shovel in 40 cm** gets ×1.57–×1.65 at every bite from 0.42 up: the sweep cuts to
  the ground, and the bot's sweep crosses about 90 L of 40 cm snow (a 46 cm blade, about half a
  metre of fresh face). Its §8.1 load was capacity-limited (20 of 30 strokes full at 65 L), so
  doubling the capacity outgrew the sweep. No bite can fix that; a longer or wider sweep would. A
  player who keeps sweeping until the load bar is full gets the full 130 L. *Reading for the
  coordinator.*
- **The large scraper in 40 cm**: ×1.71–×1.83 at every bite from 0.20 up (noise). Its 40 cm stroke
  is half capacity-limited (12–20 of 30 full).
- **The sleigh's 0.90:** with a smaller bite the cut leaves a remainder that the bot keeps sweeping
  (it aims at the nearest snow over 1 cm) instead of fresh snow. From 0.9 a pass takes the 80 cm,
  and the sleigh's own spoil on top of it, to the ground. A sleigh pass cutting 90 cm is a lot, but
  the sleigh is the big scoop, and it never fills 800 L in a stroke anyway.
- **What a bigger bite changes:**
  - In snow deeper than the old bite, one pass cuts deeper (a deeper trench), and digging in place
    takes its bite from fewer cells.
  - Between the old and the new bite, a pass now cuts to the ground.
  - In snow no deeper than the old bite, nothing changes.

**The load on the blade** (`ToolController._update_load_visual`, first and third person):
- Round 5: a full load heaped `sqrt(capacity ÷ tray)` high, clamped to 0.8–1.4, about as wide as
  the blade (8 % wider at its corners) and a fixed length along it (the sleigh ×1.6).
- **Now (amended after review)** twice the load makes twice the mound on every tool:
  - **Height:** the same `sqrt(capacity ÷ tray)`, clamped to `HEAP_MIN` 1.131 – `HEAP_MAX` 1.98
    (round 5's 0.8 and 1.4, both ×√2). With twice the capacity that is exactly √2 round 5's height
    on every tool, whether a clamp binds or not.
  - **Length** along the blade: `LOAD_LENGTH` 1.414 (√2) × round 5's. On a blade it grows forward
    from where round 5's began (a shovel's back wall, a pusher's or a scraper's face), away from
    the handle and the camera; in the sleigh's 0.9 m tub it grows both ways and still fits.
  - **Width:** round 5's scale across the blade, with the mesh turned (second pass). The mound's
    big sphere is a low-poly octagon round; round 5's had a corner at each side, so at its widest
    the mound was 1.08 × the blade's width (half-width 0.216 × width ÷ 0.4). That corner reached
    1.4–3.0 cm past the shovels', the pusher's and the snow scraper's edges, and on the sleigh it
    poked 3.2 cm through each side wall of the tub below the rim: small white nubs outside the tub
    from the third-person camera looking down, in round 5 as in the first amended version.
  - Turned half a segment (22.5°), a flat side faces each edge instead: at its widest the mound
    is 0.998 × the blade's width, inside every blade's edges and, on the sleigh, ending inside the
    tub's walls (0.399 m of the walls' outer faces at 0.400). The volume and footprint area are
    unchanged; the mound is 8 % narrower at its widest and as much shorter at its ends, so
    `LOAD_HALF_LEN` is 0.1663 (0.18 × cos 22.5°) and the forward growth still starts where round
    5's mound began. A one-line change to the mesh round 5 built (`ToolController.setup`).
  - So a full load's mound is ×2.00 round 5's on all six tools, at base and fully upgraded.
  - Seen from above it covers more of the screen (white pixels around the tool in the −30° shots,
    the tool's empty shot subtracted, against round 5's): small shovel ×1.38, standard ×1.35,
    wide pusher ×1.38, snow scraper ×1.33, large scraper ×1.39, sleigh ×1.47 (the frame's bottom
    edge cuts it).

## Readings taken

- **"About 2× per stroke" is measured with the grind_bench bot's stroke**, as the brief asks: a
  ±0.3 rad sweep of at most 40 frames, stopping when full. A stroke is limited by the snow that
  sweep crosses, so the per-stroke ratio depends on the bot's sweep as well as the tool. A player
  who keeps sweeping until the load bar is full gets the whole doubled load in any depth (the
  capacity and the fill rate are ×2; `test_s13`'s `_s13_fill` fills the doubled capacities, see
  *Tests*).
- **The bites are the smallest tested** that bring the 80 cm stroke to about ×1.8. Between runs the
  same bite varies by ±0.08 (relaxation's time budget), so the choice is to about one step of the
  table.
- **Upgrades stay multiplicative on the new base.** Only bare hands' capacity steps changed, since
  bare hands are the item the ladder broke for. Every hand tool's level keeps +25 %.
- **The load visual** keeps round 5's rule for the height (capacity relative to the blade's tray),
  with both clamps ×√2, and runs √2 longer: twice round 5's mound for twice its load, on every
  tool, and no wider than the blade. A fully upgraded tool's load heaps higher still, up to
  `HEAP_MAX`.
- **"Visibly bigger"** is judged on the shots and measured two ways: the mound's size against

  is in the two directions first person sees: higher, and longer away from the camera.

  plane outside the view (above it, looking straight down, as round 5's sleigh load did), never
  inside it. The distance rules stay as well.
- **The mound is turned, not narrowed,** to keep it inside the blade's edges and the sleigh tub's
