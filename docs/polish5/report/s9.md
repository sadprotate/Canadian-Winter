## 9.4 Fast-forward

**Substeps.** `Clock.speed` (1, 2, 10; never saved) is the speed chosen. Each frame
`Clock._process` runs `effective_speed()` substeps of the same size (`delta ×
GAME_MIN_PER_REAL_SEC`): every extra one is the clock step followed by what the rest of a frame at
1× runs for the world on the clock — `Boxes.sim_step` (box measuring), then the world's tick
(`game_root.sim_tick`: the parked vehicles' snow, `SnowRenderer.sim_step` = snowfall syncing and
relaxation, `GraderNPC.sim_step`) — and the frame's own step comes last, followed by the frame's own
box measuring and world update. So the order of everything on the clock is the same at any speed
(at 1× a frame is: grader (physics), clock, boxes, world; an extra substep: clock, boxes, world,
grader). The player, tools, vehicles, physics, animations and meshes run once per frame as before.
The per-frame code was split out of `_process` / `_physics_process` unchanged
(`Boxes.sim_step`, `SnowRenderer.sim_step`, `GraderNPC.sim_step`, `game_root._vehicle_snow_step`).
The loop re-reads `effective_speed()` after every substep and stops early when it drops (a trigger,
a mover coming near, a cap) — the remaining substeps run in later frames, so the sequence is
unchanged.

**The control.** `scripts/ui/speed_control.gd` (`SpeedControl`, new): 1×, 2×, 10× and the key
(`[T]`, from the input map) in a panel just right of the HUD clock, on its own canvas layer
(`game_root.time_layer`, layer 6) so it stays clickable over the computer's, the map's and the TV's
dim; it hides under the fade (sleep, loading), dialogs and menus. The speed the clock runs at is
filled orange; the speed chosen, when a cap or a hold keeps the clock slower, is outlined. The
buttons take the mouse only while the cursor is free (`Game.input_captured()` false), never the
keyboard focus. A line under them: "Max 2× · snowing", "Max 1× · blizzard", "Max 2× · grader on the
streets", "Max 2× · snow settling", "Held at 1× · grader passing" / "Gerry walking", and for 4 s
"Back to 1× · weather alert / contract warning / bid result / you're getting cold / working the snow
/ vehicle / machine"; "Fast-forward: on foot, indoors or at the computer" when the key or a click is
refused.

**The key.** `fast_forward` ("Fast-forward 1× / 2× / 10×"), default **T** (Decision 2: T was free
on foot — it is the vehicle radio's key, a different context, as H is the pouch and the horn), foot
context, read on foot and at the computer (`Settings.live_actions("computer")` now has it), capped
like every world-changing press (`RATE_LIMITED`, `accept_press`). It's handled in
`game_root._unhandled_input` after the `Game.typing()` return, before the modal check (so it works
at the computer), and not in a vehicle (there T stays the radio). No conflict in any place; no
migration needed (a new action; `CONTROLS_VERSION` unchanged).

**Where.** `game_root.ff_unavailable()`: on foot, indoors, or with the computer, map, TV, letter
or photo open (`FF_MODALS`); not in a vehicle or at a machine, while Gerry talks (his HUD lines),
with any other modal (his dialogue, the sleep prompt, pause, F1, the winter summary), while sleeping
(`_sleeping`, `Clock.skipping`), dead. Unavailable → back to 1× (and it stays 1×).

**Back to 1×** — `Clock.drop_to_1x(reason)`, the one hook, called from: `Weather._alert` (every
weather alert), `Contracts.on_advance` (the 12 h and 3 h warnings) and `_lose`, `Contracts._resolve_bids`
(each bid won or lost), `Survival.on_advance` (the "You are cold / dangerously cold …" warning),
`ToolController` (an accepted press that starts a scoop or a push outdoors, and every throw),
`Vehicle.enter_vehicle`, `WalkBehind.interact`, `game_root.open_modal` (a dialog), `_do_sleep`.
**For §10:** call `Clock.drop_to_1x("bid")` when a bid is won or lost at 9:01 p.m. and
`Clock.drop_to_1x("contract")` for each deadline warning (and a miss); `Clock.speed_changed(speed,
reason)` is the signal if anything else must react.

**Scripted movers.** The grader is on the city's clock: its step runs once per clock substep, so
away from the player it covers its route N× faster with the same blade steps and leaves exactly
the snow it leaves at 1× (9.5). Within `game_root.FF_HOLD_DIST` (40 m) of the player fast-forward is
held at 1× for as long as it's near (`ff_hold()`; the chosen speed comes back after). Gerry walking
within 40 m holds it too (in practice whenever he walks: the player is near), and his lines make it
unavailable. §11's sidewalk crew joins `ff_hold()` / `ff_cap()` the same way.

**Not saved.** `Clock.reset()` (new game, load) and the world's `_ready` / `_exit_tree` set 1×;
nothing about the speed is written to the save.

## Readings taken

- **"Felt" rates** are Survival's warmth rates (lost, regained, the pouch) and the HUD's warmth
  arrows; the house's own heating and cooling (power back / cut) are world rates (Decision 3 lists
  "house cooling" as world), so they stay in game time.
- **The pouch's +20 %/h** was hard-coded in `survival.gd`; it's now the balance value
  `pouch_warmth_per_hour` (10), so the felt rates are all in balance data.
- **"Clickable when the cursor is free"**: with a modal open (computer, map, TV, letter, photo) the
  cursor is free and the control is on top; on foot the mouse looks around (captured), so the key is
  the way. The control is shown greyed in a vehicle or at a machine, hidden under dialogs.
- **Available "indoors / at the computer"** also covers the map, TV, letter and photo (you're on
  foot); a dialog = Gerry's dialogue, the sleep prompt, pause, F1, the summary.
- **Starting to scoop / push** counts an accepted press outdoors (indoors the tools don't touch the
  snow, §3); a throw is every release that throws.
- **A lost contract** also drops to 1× (it's the end of the warnings).
- **"Near the player"** = 40 m (the grader is hidden behind nothing; at 40 m it is a few seconds from
  you at its 6.5 m/s). The hold is temporary (the chosen speed resumes), unlike the triggers.
- **Caps** are by the snowfall now (cm per game hour), plus two the world measures (the grader out;
  a substep's relaxation over 0.5 ms). They're temporary and shown; they only change how many
  substeps a frame runs, never what a substep does.
- **Identity** is tested with relaxation's 2.5 ms time budget off (tile count only) in all runs —
  it's the one CPU-dependent part of the snow: when it binds, two runs at 1× on different machines
  differ too. The round-robin cursors (`Boxes._cursor`, `Snow.sync_cursor`) aren't saved and carry
  over between loads in one process; the test starts each run from 0.

## Left open

- **The grader near the player** holds the clock at 1×: from the player's front yard, 1,382 frames
  (23 real seconds) of its 3-minute pass (1,176 m of lanes at 6.5 m/s) in the identity test. After
  a pass its windrows keep slumping for a while (relaxation; ~10 real seconds after a batch pass),
  during which 10× runs at 2× ("snow settling").
- **Relaxation's time budget** (2.5 ms per call) makes the snow CPU-dependent whenever it binds — as
  between two 1× runs on different machines. Fast-forward doesn't change that; the identity test
  turns the budget off for all runs.
- **10× is for clear or nearly clear weather** (below 0.5 cm/h): above it the frame budget doesn't
  hold on this VM. A cheaper snowfall sync (it is 0.5 ms a chunk in GDScript) would lift that cap;
  §9 leaves the snow sim alone.
- **The computer's monitor** re-renders once per shown game minute: 8 times a real second at 10×
  (not measured headless).
- **Other §13-style notes, not changed:** the HUD clock (0.2 s refresh) jumps 1–2 game minutes per
  update at 10×; tools lying on the ground re-settle on the snow once every 1.5 real seconds (not on
  the clock), so under a fast snowfall at 10× they sit a little low until the next check.
