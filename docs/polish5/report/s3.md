## 3.1 Walls

### The bedroom crack — found

The diagnosis is a geometric audit of the interior (the test's probe set, run against the baseline
build): short segments that must hit the house's own mesh — along each wall just in front of it,
across every wall it meets at each of the 9 inside corners (floor, eye height, under the ceiling),
and down/up through every wall-to-floor / wall-to-ceiling seam and across the wall just above the
floor and just below the ceiling at 23 points: 146 probes per house. Baseline: **9 open probes in
each house, all from two slots**; everything else closed. Both slots are in `HouseBuilder._hollow_walls`,
so both houses had them:

1. **Bedroom front-left corner** (partition meets the outer side wall): the living-room / bedroom
   partition started at `BODY_X0 + WALL_T` (−9.03) while the outer wall's room face is at
   `BODY_X0 + 0.165` (−9.085) — a 5.5 × 12 cm slot, floor to ceiling, between the bedroom and the
   living room (seen edge-on from both rooms).
2. **Bedroom front-right corner** (the T where the bedroom / office wall meets the partition): that
   wall began at `pz + WALL_T/2` (5 cm behind the partition's bedroom face) and the partition stopped
   at the wall's centre line (x −2.8): a 5 × 12 cm slot straight through the T, floor to ceiling —
   from the bedroom you saw the office floor and wall through it (`before_corner_in_*.jpg`), and from
   the office the bedroom (`before_corner_out_*.jpg`). This is the crack the playtester saw.

The outer corners (walls built in two layers that overlap, plus the corner trim boards outside),
the floor (it runs under the walls to their centre lines) and the ceiling (it sits on the walls'
tops) had no gap. **Fix:** two painted filler pieces (visual only, no collider change, no wall
moved): one from inside the outer wall to the partition's end, one post filling the T from the
partition's living-room face to the bedroom wall's start. After: 0 open probes in both houses.

Reading: "the bedroom corner from inside and outside" = from inside the bedroom and from the room on
the other side of that wall (the office), where the slot showed through; the outside corners of the
house had no gap.

## 3.2 No snow work from inside

### Diagnosis (baseline, `diag_base.log`)

Standing in the bedroom with the standard shovel, aiming out of the back window, the bedroom side
window, and at the solid back wall: **65 L scooped every time** (outdoor snow −0.065 m³), the
indicator showing. It isn't the window: the aim point comes from `Snow.raycast`, a march against the
snow heightfield that knows nothing of walls (building cells have surface height 0, so the ray goes
straight through the wall to the snow 2.7–4.2 m out — a physics ray from the eye hits the house at
0.51 m); the reach check is a horizontal distance, also blind to walls. The cut, the RMB blade, the
electric shovel's intake and a walk-behind blower held indoors (its collider is off while held) all
work on whatever cells their footprint covers beyond the wall. The same test as the new one, run on
the baseline build: at every window, the open door and the doorway of both houses, 8–10 of the 10 tools moved
outdoor snow (24 of 24 checks fail; `diag_inside_base.log`).

### The rule

* **`Buildings`** (`scripts/world/buildings.gd`, new): every building as a volume — footprint × the
  top of its roof: each built lot's house body (5.3 m; two-storey 7.6 m) and attached garage
  (4.4 m), the school, the store, the church. `register(id, rect, top, kind)` adds one (§16);
  §15's garage is the house garage, already listed. `indoors(feet)`: within a footprint or its
  walls (`SKIN` 0.12 m — a doorway counts as inside). `blocks(eye, target, list, end_skin)`: the
  segment passes through a volume.
* **Hand tools and the electric shovel** (`ToolController`): `work_ok = not Buildings.indoors(feet)`
  each frame. Indoors: no cut, no push, no intake, no indicator, no throw (a release keeps the
  load, "You can't throw snow from inside."); a press says "You can't work the snow from inside."
  Outdoors, the line test from the player's eye (`head`, also in third person): per cut cell for the
  scoop (a cell the eye only reaches through a building gets no bite; also for the preview ring), and
  for the blade / intake at its near edge with a 0.35 m allowance (`REACH_SKIN`), so a blade pressed
  against a wall from outside still works its foot.
* **Walk-behind blowers**: the same two checks on the player and the intake mouth: no intake, no
  discharge, no indicator.
* Outside next to a wall everything works as before (tested for all 10 tools at both houses).

## 3.3 Thrown snow and buildings

### Diagnosis (baseline, `diag_base.log`; 0.05 m³ thrown at each, then the depth change)

| Target | Where it went | Volume |
|---|---|---|
| Walls, windows, garage doors (both houses), school / store / church walls | the physics ray hits the wall; it "drops at its base": the deposit cone is centred on the hit point on the wall, its cells inside the footprint are skipped, so everything lands in the half-cone outside (centroid 0.1–0.3 m out) | conserved |
| A house roof (front, high throw) | a `RoofCatcher` area: the volume joins the roof's own snow layer (`roof_extra`), which is **not** part of the snow on the ground and decays 2 cm a day | **lost from the ground snow** (0.05 m³ → roof layer) |
| The open front door (player's house) | through the doorway, it lands on the floor collider *inside*; the deposit has no outdoor cell in its cone, so `_spill` rings outward and spreads it over the nearest outdoor cells all round (44 cells, ~1 mm each) | conserved, but it "disappears" |
| Gerry's open door | hit Gerry on his porch (he stands there at the opening) | conserved |

A deposit discards nothing it can place (`deposit` only returns less when the whole map is at the
ceiling; `ThrownSnow` ignores the return); no snow ever lands on building cells.

### The rule

`Throw.compute` (used by hands, every tool, the electric shovel, both walk-behind blowers and the
vehicle blowers) collects the buildings near the throw's path and, step by step, tests whether the
arc enters one (footprint + 0.12 m, from the ground to the roof top) — or whether the physics ray
hits a wall within that skin or a roof catcher. If so, the throw **stops where its path crosses that
building's outer face** (on the arc, at whatever height it is there — above the eave for a roof) and
the landing is **the foot of that wall**: the face's point moved 0.25 m out from the footprint line,
kept 15 cm in from the corners, at the snow surface there. The clump flies to the face and drops
straight down the wall (`ThrownSnow`: `stop` / `stop_time`). The deposit is the ordinary one at that
foot (cells inside the footprint skipped), so the volume is conserved exactly, nothing lands inside,
and the indicator — which shows `landing.pos` — is where it lands (≤ 0.08 m in the tests). Roofs no
longer swallow snow (`roof_extra` is no longer fed by throws; its code path stays for anything
unattributed).

Found and fixed on the way (both needed for the indicator to agree):
* The walk-behind blower's landing ring was never visible: the blower shows it from its
  `_physics_process`, the player's `ToolController` (processed after it, later in the tree) hid it in
  the same frame for any non-foot state. Now the controller leaves it to the machine, which hides it
  when it isn't running or is let go.
* Switching tools kept the previous tool's `landing`: the electric shovel threw its first bite
  (≈1 L) to wherever the last hand-tool throw had been aimed. A tool switch now clears it.

Known, left: a walk-behind blower keeps the snow it took in since its last 0.18 s discharge
(`_acc`) when it stops, and throws it on its next run (not saved); the tests count it.
